      <p class="ldvp__eyebrow">Frequently asked questions</p>
      <h2 class="ldvp__h2">Questions about wrongful death claims and hiring a lawyer</h2>
      <details><summary>Who can file a wrongful death claim in California?</summary>
        <p>California limits who can bring the claim. Generally it is the surviving spouse or domestic partner and children. If none of them survives, it can be the people who would inherit under California law. In some situations, parents, stepchildren or others who depended on the person can qualify, and the personal representative of the estate can bring the claim for the family. Who qualifies depends on your family, and the free review checks it.</p></details>
      <details><summary>How long do we have to bring a wrongful death claim?</summary>
        <p>For many California wrongful death lawsuits, the general deadline is two years from the date of death. That is not a universal rule. If a government agency may be involved, an administrative claim can be required much earlier, generally within six months. Different facts can create different deadlines, so do not use the general two-year period to assume your own.</p></details>
      <details><summary>What can a wrongful death claim cover?</summary>
        <p>In general, it can include funeral and burial costs, the income and support the person would have provided, and the loss of companionship, guidance and care. What applies depends on the facts and on who brings the claim. Landver Law does not promise any amount.</p></details>
      <details><summary>Does the death have to happen at the scene?</summary>
        <p>No. A death that happens days or weeks after an accident such as a crash can still be reviewed. The date of death and the cause matter, so tell us what happened and when.</p></details>
      <details><summary>What if my loved one was partly at fault?</summary>
        <p>Partial responsibility does not automatically mean there is no claim. California uses comparative fault: if a person's own actions contributed, damages can be reduced by that person's percentage of responsibility. Do not assume the insurance company's first fault assessment is the final one.</p></details>
      <details><summary>The other side's insurance company called me. What should I do?</summary>
        <p>First identify who is calling and which insurer they represent. Avoid guessing about facts you do not know or signing releases you do not understand. If you are unsure about a recorded statement or an offer, consider getting legal advice before agreeing to it.</p></details>
      <details><summary>Is a criminal case the same as a wrongful death claim?</summary>
        <p>No. A criminal case, if there is one, is separate from a civil claim by the family. Tell us if police or prosecutors are involved.</p></details>
      <details><summary>How much does a wrongful death lawyer cost?</summary>
        <p>The initial case review is free, and there is no fee unless we win. Landver Law handles personal-injury matters on a contingency basis, advances litigation costs and collects its legal fee when a recovery is obtained. Exact financial terms are confirmed in the written attorney-client agreement before representation begins.</p></details>
      <details><summary>How much is a wrongful death case worth?</summary>
        <p>No honest number can be named without the facts. It depends on who is bringing the claim, the losses involved, who was at fault and the insurance coverage available. The review shows what applies to your case. Landver Law does not promise a result.</p></details>
      <details><summary>What if I think a medical error caused the death?</summary>
        <p>This page is about deaths caused by accidents. It is not for medical malpractice, hospital or nursing home cases. Those claims follow different rules and often have much shorter deadlines, so do not wait to get advice elsewhere.</p></details>
