The important starting point is understanding what happened, what treatment has occurred so far and what evidence may need to be preserved.</p></details>
      <details><summary>What if I may have been partly responsible for the accident?</summary>
        <p>Partial responsibility does not automatically mean there is no claim. California uses comparative fault. If an injured person's own negligence contributed to the harm, damages can be reduced according to that person's percentage of responsibility. Do not assume the insurance company's initial fault assessment is necessarily the final allocation of responsibility.</p></details>
      <details><summary>The insurance company is asking for a recorded statement. What should I do?</summary>
        <p>First identify who is requesting the statement and which insurer they represent. Obligations can differ depending on whether the request comes from your own insurer or another party's insurer. Avoid guessing about facts you do not know, minimizing injuries that are still being evaluated or signing releases you do not understand. If you are unsure about a recorded statement or release, consider getting legal advice before agreeing to it.</p></details>
      <details><summary>I already received a settlement offer. Can I still have the case reviewed?</summary>
        <p>If you have not finalized the settlement and signed a release, you can still ask for legal advice about the situation. An early offer may arrive before the long-term medical picture, future treatment, income loss or permanent limitations are fully understood. If a release has already been signed, tell the lawyer immediately because that can materially change the analysis.</p></details>
      <details><summary>How long do I have to bring an injury claim in California?</summary>
        <p>For many California personal-injury lawsuits, the general deadline is two years from the date of injury. But that is not a universal rule. Different facts can create different deadlines, and claims involving a California government agency can require an administrative claim much earlier. For many injury claims against a California government agency, the administrative claim deadline is generally six months from the injury. Do not use the general two-year period to assume your own deadline.</p></details>
