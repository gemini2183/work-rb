- Шлем: Vehicle Code 21212 — обязателен для едущих на велосипеде младше 18 лет (штраф до $25, у родителей солидарная ответственность). Взрослых эта статья не касается. Правила для электровелосипедов и местные правила я не проверял и в текст не включил.
- Права велосипедиста: Vehicle Code 21200 — на дороге велосипедист имеет те же права и обязанности, что и водитель, кроме очевидно неприменимых.
- Положение на проезжей части: Vehicle Code 21202 — едущий медленнее потока держится «как можно ближе к правому краю, насколько практично», с исключениями: обгон, подготовка к левому повороту, опасность на краю (предметы, машины, покрытие, узкая полоса), место, где разрешён правый поворот.
- Велополоса: Vehicle Code 21208 — при наличии велополосы медленный велосипедист едет по ней, но может покинуть её для обгона, левого поворота, ради обхода мусора и опасных условий, перед правым поворотом, предварительно убедившись, что это безопасно.
- Правый поворот через велополосу («right hook»): Vehicle Code 21717 — водитель, которому нужно пересечь велополосу, обязан сначала въехать в неё и только потом поворачивать (по Vehicle Code 22100).
- Обгон: Vehicle Code 21760 (Three Feet for Safety Act) — не менее трёх футов между любой частью машины и любой частью велосипеда или велосипедиста; если нельзя, замедлиться и обгонять, только если это не опасно. Штраф $35, а при травме велосипедиста $220.
- Открытая дверь (dooring): Vehicle Code 22517 — нельзя открывать дверь на стороне, открытой для движения, пока это не безопасно и не мешает движению. ### 2. Что страховые говорят велосипедисту
**H2:** What insurance companies often tell a cyclist after a crash

1. **«You weren't riding where you should have been.»** Bicyclists on the road generally have the same rights as drivers. The law asks a cyclist who is slower than traffic to ride as close to the right edge as practicable, with exceptions, for example to avoid hazards or narrow lanes, to pass, or to prepare for a left turn. Where there is a bike lane, there are exceptions too. Whether you were where the law allows depends on the facts.
2. **«You weren't wearing a helmet.»** California's bicycle helmet law covers riders under 18. Riding without a helmet does not automatically mean there is no claim, but an insurer may argue it made your injuries worse, especially head injuries. Tell the attorney exactly what happened.
3. **«The driver says they never saw you.»** That is an explanation, not a legal conclusion. Drivers are expected to watch for bicycles too, and generally must leave at least three feet when passing one. Who was at fault depends on what happened: where each of you was, speeds, signals, witnesses and video.
4. **«Here is a quick offer for your bike and bills.»** If you sign a release, the claim may be closed before the full medical picture is known.

Подпись под блоком: California reduces compensation by your percentage of fault. It does not automatically cancel it, and the insurer's first number is not always the final one.

### 3. Что делать и чего не делать
**H2:** After a bicycle crash: what to do now, and what to hold off on

**Do now**
- Get medical care. Tell the doctor everything that hurts, even if it seems small, and say if you hit your head.
- Report the crash and save the police report number. If the driver left, report it right away and write down anything you remember: color, make, part of the plate, direction.
- Take photos of the scene, the bike lane or road, the damage to your bike and your injuries if you can.
- Keep your bike, helmet, clothing and gear as they are. A damaged helmet and bike can show how the crash happened.
- If you track rides with a phone app or bike computer, do not delete the ride. It may help show where you were riding.
- Write down names and phone numbers of witnesses.

**Hold off until you have talked to an attorney**
- Repairing, replacing or throwing out the bike or the helmet. If an insurer asks to inspect them, ask before you hand anything over.
- Giving a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer.
- Signing a release or accepting a quick offer, including an offer just to replace the bike.
- Posting about the crash on social media or in a ride app.

### 10. Вопросы
**H2:** Questions about bicycle accidents and hiring a lawyer

**What if I wasn't wearing a helmet?**
Not wearing a helmet does not automatically end a claim. California's bicycle helmet law covers riders under 18. Even where a helmet is not required, an insurer may argue that not wearing one made your injuries worse, especially head injuries. How much it matters, if at all, depends on your injuries and how the crash happened. Tell the attorney exactly what happened, including the helmet. It is better to know now than to be surprised later.

**The driver says "I never saw you." Does that help them?**
It is an explanation, not a legal conclusion. Drivers are expected to watch for bicycles. California law generally requires a driver passing a bicycle to leave at least three feet, and a driver who needs to cross a bike lane to turn generally must move into the lane first. Whether that was done depends on what happened: where each of you was, speeds, signals, witnesses and video where it exists. Tell us what you remember and what evidence may exist.

**The insurer says I was riding in the wrong place. Can I still have a claim?**
Possibly. Bicyclists on the road generally have the same rights as drivers. The law asks cyclists to ride as close to the right edge as practicable, but lists exceptions, for example to avoid hazards, to pass, to prepare for a left turn, or where the lane is too narrow to share safely. Where there is a bike lane, there are exceptions too. California uses comparative fault: if a cyclist's own actions contributed to the crash, damages can be reduced by the cyclist's percentage of responsibility, not automatically denied. A free case review looks at what happened in your crash.

**A car door opened into me. Who is responsible?**
California law says a person may not open a vehicle door on the side open to moving traffic unless it is reasonably safe and does not interfere with that traffic. An insurer may still argue about where you were riding or how fast. Who was responsible depends on the facts, such as how close you were to the parked car and whether the person looked before opening the door. Take photos of the door, the lane and your bike, and keep the bike as it is.

**The driver left the scene. Can I still do anything?**
Report it to the police right away, even if you are hurt and the driver is gone. Write down what you remember (color, make, part of the plate, direction, where it happened) and look for witnesses and nearby cameras. Whether insurance can help depends on the facts and the policies involved, and some reporting deadlines can be short, so do not wait. A free case review can help you understand what may apply.

**The other driver's insurance company called me. What should I do?**
First identify who is calling and which insurer they represent. Avoid guessing about facts you do not know, minimizing injuries that are still being evaluated, or signing releases you do not understand. If you are unsure about a recorded statement or an offer, consider getting legal advice before agreeing to it.
