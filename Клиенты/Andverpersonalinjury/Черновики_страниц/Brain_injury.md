### 2. Что говорят после травмы головы
**H2:** What insurance companies often say after a head injury

1. **«It's only a mild concussion. It will pass.»** Doctors use «mild» for a concussion-type injury. It does not tell you how you will feel next month. CDC says most people feel better within a couple of weeks. If you do not, tell your doctor.
2. **«Your scan was normal, so you're fine.»** A CT scan or MRI often looks normal after a concussion. A normal scan does not by itself rule the injury out. Your symptoms, exams and your doctor's notes matter.
3. **«Your symptoms started later, so it's not from the crash.»** CDC says some symptoms may not appear for hours or days. An insurer may still argue that the injury came from something else. See a doctor and write down the date each symptom began.
4. **«Here is a quick offer.»** If you sign a release, the claim may be closed before it is clear how the injury will affect work, memory and daily life.

Подпись под блоком: California reduces compensation by your percentage of fault. It does not automatically cancel it, and the insurer's first number is not always the final one.

### 3. Что делать и чего не делать
**H2:** After a head injury: what to do now, and what to hold off on

**Do now**
- See a doctor, even if you feel mostly fine. Say how it happened and describe every symptom: headache, dizziness, memory, focus, mood, sleep, vision, sensitivity to light or noise.
- Go to emergency care right away for warning signs such as a headache that keeps getting worse, repeated vomiting, confusion, seizures or passing out. CDC lists them.
- Write a short note each day: date, symptoms, what you could not do (work, driving, reading). A family member can help.
- Keep every record: ER and urgent care papers, discharge instructions, work notes, bills. Go to your follow-up visits.
- Take photos of the place and any injuries. Save witness names and the police or incident report number. If you fell on someone's property, report it to the owner or manager and ask for a copy.

**Hold off until you have talked to an attorney**
- Giving a recorded statement to an insurer.
- Telling an insurer you are «fine» or «recovered» while symptoms are still being checked.
- Signing a release or accepting a quick offer.
- Posting about the accident or your recovery on social media.

Подпись: This is general information, not legal advice or medical advice.

### 10. Вопросы
**H2:** Questions about brain injuries and hiring a lawyer

**I feel mostly fine, but my head, memory or mood is not right. Is it too early to worry?**
Not necessarily. According to CDC, concussion symptoms can affect how you feel, think, sleep and react emotionally, and some may not appear for hours or days. This is not a diagnosis. If something feels off, tell your doctor and write down what you notice. A free case review looks at what happened and what has been documented so far.

**My symptoms started days after the accident. Does that hurt my case?**
It can raise questions, and an insurer may argue that the symptoms came from something else. Delayed symptoms are not unusual, according to CDC. What helps is seeing a doctor, telling them how the injury happened, and noting the date each symptom began. An attorney can look at the timeline in your case.

**The insurer says it was just a mild concussion. What does that mean?**
«Mild» is a medical term for a concussion-type injury. It does not describe how the injury may affect your work, sleep, memory or mood. How an injury affects you is something your doctors can assess. Avoid guessing, and do not tell an insurer you are fully recovered while your symptoms are still being checked.

**My CT scan or MRI was normal. Can I still have a claim?**
Possibly. A concussion often does not show up on a CT scan or MRI, so a normal scan does not by itself mean there is no injury. A claim is generally looked at through your medical records, your symptoms, exams by doctors and how your daily life has changed. An attorney can explain what documentation may matter in your case.

**The other side's insurance company called me. What should I do?**
First identify who is calling and which insurer they represent. Avoid guessing about facts you do not know, saying you are fine while symptoms are still being evaluated, or signing releases you do not understand. If you are unsure about a recorded statement or an offer, consider getting legal advice before agreeing to it.

**My family member was hurt and cannot handle this. What can we do?**
Call us, or leave a number. A family member or caregiver can start the conversation. How someone can act for a person who cannot make their own decisions depends on the situation, and an attorney can explain the options. There is no obligation.

**I fell on someone else's property, or I was attacked. Can the property owner be responsible?**
Possibly. An owner or manager may be responsible when unsafe conditions or poor security played a part in an injury. It depends on the facts: what was wrong, what the owner knew or should have known, and what evidence exists. Report what happened, take photos if you can, and keep any names and reports.
